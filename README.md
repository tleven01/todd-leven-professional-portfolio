# My Approach to Technology, Customers, and Problem Solving

### Todd Leven | Senior IT Account Executive | Public Sector / SLED | Cloud | SaaS | Cybersecurity | AI

I have spent much of my career helping customers solve complex technology and business problems. My goal is to be more than a salesperson presenting products. I want to understand the environment, ask better questions, bring the right people together, and help customers make better decisions.

Early in my career, I was impressed by the depth of knowledge strong Solutions Engineers brought to customer conversations. I did not want to become an engineer, but I did want to become a better resource to customers and a stronger partner to technical teams. That motivated me to keep expanding my own toolkit through cloud, cybersecurity, data, analytics, AI, and other technologies that help me understand problems more clearly and contribute more meaningfully to customer discussions.

## Using Data to Solve Problems

Data analysis has become an important part of how I think about customer and sales challenges. Instead of relying only on assumptions or vendor opinions, data can help identify patterns, compare options, uncover root causes, and focus the conversation on the issues that matter most.

For example, in a complex cybersecurity environment, data can help identify overlapping tools, unnecessary spending, support trends, underused capabilities, and gaps in coverage. That creates a more objective discussion about what should stay, what should change, and where the customer can reduce risk, complexity, or cost.

The same thinking applies to territory management. CRM history, renewal dates, installed technologies, purchasing patterns, budget cycles, support activity, and pipeline aging can help identify which accounts deserve attention, where growth opportunities may exist, and which deals are losing momentum.

I am currently nearing completion of the Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate to strengthen my ability to use data more effectively in sales, consulting, and business decision-making.

## Using AI to Improve Preparation and Decision-Making

I use AI as a research and thinking tool. It can help me research accounts, identify likely priorities, compare technologies, prepare discovery questions, organize information, and develop stronger account plans.

I do not see AI as a replacement for judgment or customer conversation. I see it as a way to become better prepared, ask better questions, and make more thoughtful recommendations.

## Helping Customers and Colleagues

Whether I am working with a customer or a colleague, I try to start by understanding the real problem. I look at what we know, what we may be assuming, what information is missing, and what resources should be involved.

I believe the best outcomes usually come from combining strong customer relationships, technical expertise, data, business judgment, and collaboration.

## Mentoring

I also enjoy mentoring and supporting others. I see mentoring as a collaborative process built around listening, asking thoughtful questions, sharing relevant experience, and helping someone develop their own judgment and confidence.

For me, mentoring is an opportunity to give back, make a meaningful difference, and help others recognize their own strengths and potential.

## What I Am Continually Building

I continue developing my knowledge in:

- Artificial Intelligence
- Cloud Computing
- Cybersecurity

- Process Improvement

My objective is simple: become a better resource to customers, colleagues, and the organizations I work with.

## The Principle Behind It

> Technology creates the most value when we understand the problem clearly, use evidence intelligently, involve the right people, and measure whether the outcome actually improved.
